In short.

I’ve spent most of my adult life in restaurants, and I’m good at the parts most people hate. Scheduling. Inventory. The talk with the guy who’s late again.

Hibachi line cook. Front-of-house manager. Whataburger GM-in-training running two stores. Executive assistant at a college. And now the guy who trains the new sushi hires and opens and closes the bar on his own.

I’m looking for a management role again. Full-time. Somewhere I can stay.

01

2007 → now

Bamboo Sushi & Hibachi
Niceville, FL

The place I keep coming back to.

Sushi Chef & TrainerFormer FOH ManagerFormer Line Cook

Four separate stretches across eighteen years. Hibachi line cook, front-of-house manager, sushi support, and now sushi chef and trainer. If there’s a job at Bamboo I haven’t done, it’s owning it.

  • Run sushi-bar production during service and train new hires on prep, stocking, cleaning, and service standards. About 7 to 8 people trained so far, all still speaking to me.
  • Open and close the department independently. Nobody checks my work, because nobody needs to.
  • As FOH manager, kept the front and the kitchen in sync on nights when both wanted to strangle each other.
  • Resolve customer issues before they reach a manager. Hold the line on food safety and presentation.
“Behind!” Me, roughly 400 times a shift.
02

2015 → 2018

Whataburger
Crestview & DeFuniak Springs, FL

Ninety people. Two stores. One guy with a clipboard.

ManagerGeneral Manager in Development

This is the job that proved I can run a building. Two locations, a team of about 90 employees, and every rush that a highway exit in Florida can throw at you.

  • Scheduled and managed labor for ~90 people across two stores. Hit labor-cost targets and still covered every shift.
  • Owned hiring end to end: interviews, onboarding, training, and discipline when it came to that.
  • Daily operations: safe counts, bank deposits, manager logs, and store walks for cleanliness, safety, and staffing gaps.
  • Placed weekly food and supply orders. When one store ran short, coordinated transfers with nearby locations, and yes, drove the product over myself.
  • Handled customer issues directly and kept shifts moving during high-volume rushes.
“He drove the fries to the other store himself.” Whataburger folklore. Verified.
